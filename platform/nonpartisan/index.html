Nonpartisan Government and Local Decision-Making

Finding Common Ground on Local Issues

In today's world, can anyone truly be nonpartisan?

I believe so. I do know that those tasked with making decisions have to make that effort, or little good will be accomplished by anyone.

Let's look at solar energy.

The hyper-partisan approach gives us two schools of thought. One is that solar is a waste of time and effort, ugly if you can see it, a relatively inefficient producer of power, and that we don't get much value or savings from it. The other is that renewable energy may be part of the answer for a country in search of a future with cleaner air.

The no-solar people want no solar at all, and hyper-partisan rules require them to be 100% opposed. They won't even discuss the things that might make some of their objections less troubling. The pro-solar people don't want to admit that some places are simply not suited for solar farms because that would mean speaking out against solar. Oh, noooo.

The result is that neither side does anything. We get solar farms in some really bad places, without land-use codes that encourage the best possible locations.

The central idea

Hyper-partisan political views do not have to keep us from meeting in the middle.

Solar development in Allegany County

Finding better locations for solar

Right now, our state law favors more solar. The state has gone so far as to give counties less control over where solar farms can go, and no say over whether we will have them at all.

But the reality is that solar is here, and we have to find the best and least invasive use of it.

Local land-use rules

Here in Allegany County, since we can't issue a blanket "no," our elected officials have decided not to play at all. We have one of the weakest solar codes in the state. It has not been substantially updated since 2017, and our county doesn't even bother to enforce the limited code we do have.

Property-tax revenue and local impacts

But wouldn't it be nice if we could create that property-tax revenue AND make sure solar farms are not destroying our beautiful views or increasing problems?

Views, drainage and watersheds

There is no doubt that solar development alters our landscape in ways that go beyond appearance. Depending on where and how it is built, a large solar project can alter drainage, affect watershed health, and potentially increase flooding during major storms.

A local example

Finding common ground in Cash Valley

At the PSC hearing for the solar farm proposed at Helmstetter/Cash Valley, many people who 100% support solar power because of its renewable-energy benefits spoke out against putting a solar farm in that location. They recognized that it would negatively affect an important vista appreciated by both local residents and visitors.

At the same hearing, people who have nothing good to say about solar spoke in favor of the project because it is that man's property, and they believe no one should tell him what to do with it.

In this instance, people from both sides crossed over to what is usually considered the other side. And they had to get there by going through the middle.

Wouldn't it be more productive if we could meet in the middle in the first place and feel comfortable discussing the realities without fear of being labeled a hypocrite? We would need to have discussions that don't begin and end with telling people they are wrong.

Practical local government

Making decisions beyond party lines

Doing that in Annapolis or Washington may seem nearly impossible. But we can do it here.

Our local decisions are complicated, and they need to be practical. They cannot be made in an abstract, ideological context. We need our decisions to produce results that are valuable to Allegany County, and we can't get there by being exclusively red or blue, adamant that it's "my way or the highway."

Proposed action

As your unaffiliated county commissioner, with no party organization dictating my actions or applying pressure behind closed doors, I would update our land-use code, protect important views and watersheds, and limit solar development to locations where it can produce energy and property-tax revenue with the least damage to our county and our valued way of life.
