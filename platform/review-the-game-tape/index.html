Economic Development and Government Accountability

Economic Development: It Is Time to Review the Game Tape

Most agree that economic development in Allegany County is falling short. The question voters should be asking every candidate is simple: What are you going to do about it?

I believe we can fix this. We can get better results. We can also stop wasting money that should be improving the quality of life for everyone who lives here.

How? Use a proven method. In sports, the work does not end when the game ends. Win or lose, the team reviews the tape. Coaches look for missed opportunities. They study mistakes. They ask what could have produced a better result.

The review process

Review the game tape

After watching this commission closely for the past 18 months, I believe this is where Allegany County has missed an enormous opportunity. We announce economic development deals with great enthusiasm. Then we rarely return to tell the public whether the promises were fulfilled.

As a county commissioner, I will push tirelessly for a post-game assessment of every major economic development deal made during the past six years. Some of those deals are not finished. Some are still trying to become what the County told us they would be. We need to know what is working, what is stalled and what still needs attention.

  • Establish what was promised

    What did taxpayers put into the deal? What did the County expect to receive? What did the written agreement actually require? How many jobs were promised? What wages, private investment and tax revenue were promised?

  • Compare promises with results

    What has actually been delivered? What remains unfinished? What went wrong? What can we do better next time? We cannot learn from these deals if we cannot see what was promised and what actually happened.

  • Report progress every three months

    For active projects, the Economic Development Department should provide a public progress report every three months. Give it to the commissioners. Give it to the public. Post it on the County website. These reports would tell us what is working while there is still time to act.

  • Identify problems before projects stall

    Has a company quietly put its Allegany County project on the back burner? Is it having trouble with permits, workers, infrastructure or financing? Is the County failing to provide the follow-through that a legitimate employer needs? Regular reports would help us spot problems and solve them before another project quietly fades away.

Measuring economic development

Measure what taxpayers are getting

Economic development should produce clear and measurable public benefits. A low-interest public loan is a taxpayer investment. We have a right to know what we are getting in return.

Count the full public investment

We should count the entire public investment. That includes grants, loans, land, tax breaks and infrastructure. The County should not split those costs into different categories to make the total look smaller.

Measure lasting jobs

Permanent jobs matter more than temporary construction work. Job quality matters just as much as the number of jobs. Wages, benefits and opportunities for advancement should be part of every review.

The County should disclose the cost per permanent job before approving a deal and recalculate it in every progress report.

Compare expected and actual returns

Every deal should identify the jobs, wages, private investment and tax revenue we expect. Tax revenue projections should be realistic and put in writing. Actual tax revenue should be compared with what was originally projected.

Protecting public money

Protect taxpayers and manage risk

Government should help create the conditions for private investment. It should not operate like an investment bank. Public money should not absorb risks that belong to private investors.

Set enforceable terms

Every incentive should have written goals, deadlines and reporting requirements. Agreements should include real consequences when promised jobs or investments do not happen. Grants can be structured as performance-based loans. Part of a loan can be forgiven each year, but only after that year's promises have been met and verified. Long-term agreements need a way out if the company does not perform.

Evaluate financial risks

Every agreement should clearly tell us who controls the project, who receives the money and who is responsible for the losses. Large or risky projects should receive independent financial and operational reviews before taxpayer money is committed.

Understand speculative investments

Established companies with customers, revenue and a proven product usually present less risk than speculative startups. Startups can become important employers, but we need to be honest about the risk. Venture capitalists expect several investments to fail. They are counting on one big success to cover those losses. County government cannot play that game with taxpayer money.

A speculative company should bring real private money to the table. If the owners do not have their own money in the game, why should we? Public support for a startup should be limited and provided in stages. The company should have to meet independently verified goals before receiving the next stage of support.

Supporting employers

Help businesses succeed after the deal

When my father was transferred to a new Army base, our family was connected with a sponsor family. They helped us find our way, answered questions and made us feel welcome.

We can do something similar here.

Proposed action

I would create a volunteer business welcome team for every major employer coming into the county.

A small group of community volunteers could help new business leaders and their families learn about our schools, neighborhoods, services and community organizations. They could answer questions, make introductions and help newcomers feel connected.

Economic development is not finished when an agreement is signed. We should keep asking what a company needs, what is slowing the project down and what we can reasonably do to help.

Government transparency

Demand transparency and follow-through

Confidentiality should be limited and temporary. Once negotiations are over, the agreement and the taxpayers' obligations should be public.

Commissioners should not learn about major commitments after staff has already finalized them. The public should not have to file information requests just to learn the basic terms of a completed deal.

Economic development staff should be evaluated on completed results, not announcements. Success means operating businesses, lasting jobs and a stronger tax base.

Failed or disappointing projects should be examined openly. When you hide your mistakes, you never learn from them.

The goal is not to criticize every deal that fails. Economic development will always involve some uncertainty. The goal is to become more careful with public money, more open with the public and more effective with every decision we make.

Why these changes are needed

Questions about previous county deals

Every economic development deal involving taxpayer money should teach us something. We cannot learn from these deals if the public cannot see what was promised or what actually happened.

LongHorn Steakhouse

What did taxpayers receive in exchange for the $2.8 million, 3.5%, 25-year loan connected to the LongHorn Steakhouse development?

The repayment schedule shows that the County will collect about $1.4 million in interest over 25 years. What it does not show is how much more the County could have earned at a fair market rate.

We now have the promissory note, but we still do not have the complete Loan Agreement. Without that agreement, taxpayers cannot determine what collateral secures the loan, what it is worth or where the County stands if the borrower defaults.

Luke Mill

The purchase of the Luke Mill site is approaching its second anniversary. The property is producing no property tax revenue. All demolition and salvage revenue is going to a private company.

When a deal is heavily promoted but the results are less impressive, it may be tempting to let that deal disappear from public view. That prevents accountability. It also prevents us from doing better the next time.

Review the Luke Mill records.
